Rewrite principles (apply in order):

1. Rhythm — alternate long and short; merge fragments; anchor with a short line
   - Use longer sentences to carry related context, data, and cause together — linked with "with...", "leaving...", "as...", not a pile of unrelated short lines.
   - Use a short sentence as the landing point: a judgment, conclusion, or emotional beat. The shortest line in a paragraph should often be its anchor.
   - Avoid staccato runs: three or more short sentences in a row feel breathless, not conversational. Merge at least two of them.
   - Use em dashes for breathing room; use periods to mark rhythm boundaries.
   - Bad: "It's summer. Days are long. Sunsets are late." → three fragments, rhythm collapses.
   - Good: "With summer daylight stretching past 14 hours, the late sunset has pushed your wind-down window back."

2. Narrative — build cause-and-effect; answer "so what?"
   - Before writing, identify the one thing the reader most needs to know. Every detail should serve that core point.
   - Link ideas with "so", "which means", "the result is", "leaving you..." — not a bullet-list of unrelated facts.
   - Do not say the same thing twice in different words (e.g. "system aroused" + "body still tense" → merge into one stronger line).
   - Do not pad with filler that adds no new fact from the source.

3. Word choice — body-felt language over jargon
   - Swap medical/scientific abstractions for what the reader can actually feel, without changing the underlying fact (see replacement list above).

4. Closing beat — land the paragraph, don't stop on raw data
   - For multi-sentence copy, the ending should offer a brief judgment, reassurance, or clear "what this means" — not end on a bare number.
   - Stay grounded, not sentimental. The reader should feel settled, not left hanging.
   - Echo key imagery from the paragraph in the closing phrase when natural (e.g. if the text discusses a "window" or "delay", "give yourself a little extra room" beats "a little extra time").
   - Bad ending: "...your stress score is 82."
   - Good ending: "Nothing alarming — just give yourself a little extra room to unwind tonight."
   - Skip forced closings on very short labels, titles, or single-line UI copy.

Before you return, check:
